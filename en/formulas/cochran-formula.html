Example: a poll wants to estimate the share of voters who approve of a candidate, at 95% confidence with a margin of error of 5 percentage points. With no prior estimate, p̂ = 50% is used, which gives the largest possible sample. Change the values to work out your own case.

Step by step

    What each term means

    Sample Size (n₀)
    The number of people to include in the sample to get statistically meaningful results, assuming a large or infinite population.
    Squared Z-Score (Z²)
    The critical value of the standard normal distribution for the chosen confidence level. For example, at 95% confidence, Z ≈ 1.96.
    Estimated Proportion (p̂)
    The estimated share of the population with a given characteristic. If unknown, p̂ = 0.5 is used, which maximizes the variance and gives the largest possible sample.
    Complement of the Proportion (q̂)
    Computed as (1 - p̂). It is the estimated share of the population without the characteristic.
    Squared Margin of Error (e²)
    The desired precision, also called sampling error. It is the 'window' around the estimate (e.g. ±5%).
