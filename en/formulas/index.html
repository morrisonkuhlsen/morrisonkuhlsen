Formulas

111 statistics and probability formulas explained term by term. Open one and tap each piece to see what it means.

Probability

  • Bayes' Theorem Updates the probability of a hypothesis after seeing evidence.
  • Diagnostic Test: Predictive Values What a positive is worth, depending on how rare the disease is.
  • Addition Rule (Union of Events) The chance of at least one of two events.
  • Conditional Probability The chance of A when you already know B happened.
  • Law of Total Probability Adds up the probability of B across every scenario of a partition.
  • Simpson's Paradox When the winner in every group loses overall.
  • Permutations and Arrangements Counting with or without order, with or without repetition.
  • Binomial Coefficient How many ways there are to choose k items out of n, ignoring order.
  • Birthday Problem The chance that two people in a group share a birthday.
  • Coupon Collector's Problem How many stickers complete the album, on average.
  • Monty Hall Problem Why switching doors doubles your chance of winning the car.
  • Gambler's Ruin The chance of reaching a target before going broke.
  • Benford's Law Why the first digit is so often 1.
  • Expected Value The long-run average of a random variable.
  • Shannon Entropy The average surprise of an outcome, in bits.
  • Markov's Inequality A ceiling on high values, knowing only the mean.
  • Chebyshev's Theorem A bound on the share of data far from the mean, for any distribution.

Distributions

  • Bernoulli Distribution A single trial with two outcomes: success or failure.
  • Binomial Distribution The number of successes in n independent trials.
  • Geometric Distribution How many trials until the first success.
  • Negative Binomial Distribution How many trials until the r-th success.
  • Hypergeometric Distribution Successes in a sample drawn without replacement.
  • Poisson Distribution The number of events in an interval, given a mean rate.
  • Uniform Distribution Every value in an interval equally likely.
  • Exponential Distribution The waiting time until the next event.
  • Gamma Distribution The time until the k-th event.
  • Weibull Distribution The service life of parts and equipment, and the failure rate.
  • Normal (Gaussian) Distribution The bell curve defined by its mean and standard deviation.
  • Lognormal Distribution Quantities that grow by multiplication, such as income and prices.
  • Beta Distribution The uncertainty about a proportion, updated with the data.
  • Law of Large Numbers The mean of many repetitions approaches the expected value.
  • Central Limit Theorem Why the sample mean tends to a normal distribution as n grows.

Inference

  • Z-Score How many standard deviations an observation is from the mean.
  • P-Value What it measures, what it doesn't, and the tails of the normal.
  • Student's t-Test Compares a sample mean with a hypothesized value when σ is unknown.
  • Two-Sample t-Test Compares the means of two independent groups (Welch).
  • Paired t-Test Compares before and after in the same people.
  • Mann-Whitney Test Compares two groups by ranks, without assuming normality.
  • One-Way ANOVA Compares the means of three or more groups.
  • Kruskal-Wallis Test ANOVA done on ranks, without assuming normality.
  • Multiple Comparisons and Bonferroni Why many tests find effects that aren't there.
  • Standard Error of the Mean How much the mean of a sample varies from one sample to the next.
  • Confidence Interval A range of plausible values for the population mean.
  • Confidence Interval for a Proportion The Wald and Wilson methods, and why the second is better.
  • Confidence Interval for the Variance Between what values the population standard deviation lies.
  • Bootstrap A confidence interval by resampling, with no standard-error formula.
  • Statistical Power How many observations a t-test needs to detect an effect.
  • Two Proportions How many people per group to detect a difference between two proportions.
  • Chi-Square Test (χ²) Measures the gap between observed and expected frequencies.
  • Chi-Square Test of Independence Whether two categorical variables are associated, in a contingency table.
  • McNemar Test Proportions before and after, in the same people.
  • Odds Ratio and Relative Risk How much an exposure raises the odds or the risk of an outcome.

Sampling

  • Cochran's Formula Sample size to estimate a proportion with a given margin of error.
  • Sample Size for a Mean How many observations to estimate a mean within a given margin of error.
  • Margin of Error The "plus or minus" of a poll, from the sample size.
  • Poll Simulator Draw thousands of polls and see how many intervals get it right.
  • Finite Population Correction Shrinks the sample size when the population is small.
  • Systematic Sampling Draw the start and take one every k.
  • Proportional Allocation Each stratum gets its share of the sample.
  • Neyman Allocation Splits the sample across strata by size and variability.
  • Weighted Mean and Survey Weights Means with weights, correcting polls and the effective sample size.

Association and means

  • Standard Deviation How far the values spread around the mean.
  • Coefficient of Variation The spread relative to the mean, to compare different scales.
  • Quartiles and Interquartile Range The middle half of the data, the boxplot and the outliers.
  • Skewness and Kurtosis The shape of the data: which way it leans and how heavy its tails are.
  • Covariance Whether two variables move together, and in which direction.
  • Pearson Correlation Coefficient (r) Strength and direction of a linear relationship, from −1 to 1.
  • Spearman Correlation Correlation by ranks: catches increasing relationships that aren't straight.
  • Partial Correlation The relationship between two variables, discounting a third.
  • Cohen's Kappa The agreement between two raters, net of chance.
  • Simple Linear Regression The line that predicts y from x.
  • Regression Prediction Interval Where a new value should fall — and why the band is wide.
  • Coefficient of Determination (R²) What share of the variation in y the line explains.
  • Logistic Regression The fit for yes-or-no outcomes, and the odds ratio.
  • Geometric Mean The right average for growth rates and factors that multiply.
  • Harmonic Mean The right average for rates and ratios.

Time series

  • Moving Average The trend of a series, without the short swings.
  • Exponential Smoothing Smooths and forecasts, giving more weight to recent values.
  • Holt's Linear Smoothing A forecast that tracks both level and trend.
  • Autocorrelation How much a series resembles itself k periods later.
